Why format is its own budget line

revision leaf · section
README.md:88

Format cost means only this: constraining the output shape. Input-side formatting - examples, templates, delimiters - is a separate sensitivity with its own mechanism, under attention cost.

The measured quantity is the gap between two performances on one task.

Format tax equals freeform performance minus format-constrained performance.

A positive value means demanding a shape hurt the work.

Two setups, needing names because the result hinges on their difference.

  • 1-Turn (GET·): a single generation produces the answer and the required format together, with format spec, examples, and task in the prompt.
  • 2-Turn (··T· → GET·): two generations - a freeform answer first, then a second pass reformatting it under identical format instructions.

Only when the format is applied differs, which isolates the tax. On LaTeX writing one open-weight model lost 15.7 quality points in 1-Turn and 7.0 in 2-Turn.

The fix is partial: 2-Turn recovers 8.7 of 15.7, non-uniformly, costing an extra call and roughly double tokens - mitigation, not cure.

Practical rule: keep format requests late and narrow; do not pin a heavy output schema into standing instructions, where every turn re-applies and re-charges it.

Caveats: recent closed-weight models show almost no tax - a gap open weights have not closed. One study only, so treat the magnitude as provisional.

Self-test

Define the format tax as a measured difference, explain what the 1-Turn versus 2-Turn comparison isolates about its cause, and report how much of the tax the decoupled setup recovers and at what price.

Reveal answer

Scope first: format cost means only the cost of constraining the output shape. Input-side formatting — examples, templates, delimiters — is a different sensitivity with a different mechanism, filed under attention cost. Measured quantity: format tax = Perf(freeform) − Perf(format-constrained) on the same task; a positive value means demanding a shape degraded the work. Two setups, because the result turns on their difference. 1-Turn (GET·): one generation produces the answer and the required format together, with format spec (G), examples (E), and task (T) in the prompt. 2-Turn (··T· → GET·): two generations — a freeform answer first with no format instruction at all, then a second pass reformatting it under the same format instructions. The format instructions are identical in both; only when they are applied changes, and that is precisely what isolates the tax from the content. On LaTeX writing, one open-weight model paid 15.7 quality points in 1-Turn and 7.0 in 2-Turn — recovering 8.7, just over half, non-uniformly (one model's tax grew), bought with an extra call and roughly doubled token consumption. Decoupling reasoning from formatting is a mitigation in the same currency, not a cure. Practical rule: keep format requests late and narrow; do not pin a heavy output schema into standing instructions where every turn re-applies and re-charges it. Caveats: recent closed-weight models show little to no tax, and this rests on one study — treat the magnitude as provisional.
